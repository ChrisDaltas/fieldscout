### 1.1 The clock, and what it actually costs

- **`draft_tick` is scheduled by pg_cron every 5 seconds, once, globally** — `cron.schedule('draft-tick', '5 seconds', 'SELECT public.draft_tick()')` at `supabase/migrations/068_draft_tick_autopick.sql:1265`. §22.3's rule is ONE cron entry for the whole system, never one per draft. **This sweep is not to be made faster** to solve a mock-pacing problem: it sweeps every live draft in the app.
- **A nomination opens the bid clock at `auction_bid_seconds`** — `draft_nominate` reads `v_bid_seconds := COALESCE((v_draft.config->>'auction_bid_seconds')::int, 20)` at `085_auction_nominate_bid.sql:485` and sets `current_deadline` from it.
- **Anti-snipe only re-floors a clock a bid lands inside** — `draft_place_bid`'s D128 arm, `085:778` (`v_anti_snipe := COALESCE(…, 10)`, then `GREATEST(current_deadline, now() + anti_snipe)`).
- **Therefore the dull case costs double the exciting one.** An *uncontested* nomination pays the full **20s** opening window; a *contested* one resolves on the **10s** anti-snipe reset. On the default roster — `DEFAULT_ROSTER_SETTINGS` in `src/lib/leagues/settings/league-settings.ts:164–177` gives 9 starters + 6 bench ⇒ `deriveRosterSize`/D91 **15 draftable slots** ⇒ **180 nominations** at 12 teams — the opening windows alone are `180 × 20s =` **60 minutes** before anyone types anything.
- `auction_bid_seconds` is `z.number().int().min(10).max(60).default(20)` — `league-settings.ts:222`. `auction_nomination_seconds` default 30 (`:221`), `auction_anti_snipe_seconds` default 10 (`:223`).

### 1.2 CPU bidders — where they live and how they pace

- **CPU bidders exist only in MOCK auctions.** `draft_tick`'s ARM 2.6(c) claim requires `d.is_mock AND d.draft_type = 'auction'` — `089_mock_auctions.sql:2192–2194`. A real draft has no auto-bidder at all (§8.6.5: absent managers do not auto-bid), so **Chris's <90-minute live-draft target is a clock problem, not a bot problem**; the <45-minute mock target is both.
- **One CPU raise per sweep pass per nomination, of exactly $1.** ARM 2.6(c2), `089:2313–2345`: the candidate query is `… WHERE b.open_slots >= 1 AND v_high_bid + 1 <= LEAST(c.value, b.max_bid) ORDER BY c.value DESC, c.idx LIMIT 1`, and the write is `PERFORM public.draft_place_bid_internal(v_draft.id, v_cand_team, v_high_bid + 1, NULL, 'draft_tick')`.
- **So each $1 costs 5 seconds of wall-clock.** $1 → $40 is 39 raises ≈ **3m15s** of a room where nothing visibly happens. F76's full arithmetic (≈ 3.5–4 h for a 12×15×$200 mock) is on the ledger row and is not re-derived here.
- **The value model already exists and is not the problem.** `draft_mock_cpu_bid_value` (`089:329–360`, `LANGUAGE sql IMMUTABLE STRICT`) = `4.0 × budget/slots × (1 − (rank−1)/n)³ × need × (1 + noise)`, seeded on `(draft, seq, team, pass)` — the D93 24-bit PRNG construction. On a 12×15×$200 league it prices rank 1 at ≈ $45–61 and rank 100 at ≈ $5. **A CPU already knows what a player is worth; it just refuses to say so in one breath.**
- Think-time is `draft_mock_auction_cpu_due` (`089:288`), roster need is `draft_mock_cpu_need` (`089:394`), and the one bid validator/writer is `draft_place_bid_internal` (`089:512`).
- **The extraction precedent is in the same migration.** `draft_system_nominate_internal` (`089:658`) was pulled out of 086's ARM 2.6(a) *because a second consumer appeared* (the CPU think-time nomination). ARM 2.6(b)'s award has never been extracted and is still ~150 inline lines at `089:2480–2671`.

### D198 — Retiring `auction_min_bid`: one derived reserve, one data migration, and what survives of C38

1. **The two jobs collapse into ONE derived number, read through ONE helper.** Add `draft_auction_reserve(p_config jsonb) → INTEGER` (`IMMUTABLE`, `search_path=''`): `CASE WHEN COALESCE((p_config->>'auction_zero_dollar_nominations')::boolean, FALSE) THEN 0 ELSE 1 END`. **Every** site in §1.3's table that reads `COALESCE((config->>'auction_min_bid')::int, 1)` calls it instead — no site keeps a private COALESCE, no site hardcodes `1`. This is the §4.7 one-authority discipline applied to a second quantity, and it is why the change is mechanical rather than delicate: the nomination floor and the per-slot reserve were *already* the same variable in every body, so making them one function preserves the algebra exactly at the default and moves both to 0 together under the toggle.
2. **The removal is a DATA migration, and skipping that half breaks every league that stored the key.** `league-settings.ts:213` is `z.strictObject` and `mergeSettings` parses the stored blob on every read (`:643–663`), so a leftover `settings.draft.auction_min_bid` is a hard parse failure, not a harmless extra. The migration rewrites **both** stores — `leagues.settings->'draft'` and every `drafts.config` (the draft row snapshots the catalog at create) — dropping the key and writing `auction_zero_dollar_nominations`: stored `0` ⇒ `true`, stored `≥ 1` ⇒ `false`. **It must count and RAISE NOTICE what it changed, and separately count rows where the stored value was ≥ 2** — those leagues are the only ones whose *meaning* changes (a $2–$5 reserve becomes $1), and a silent conversion of somebody's settings is precisely the failure CLAUDE.md's "never let nothing happened mean it worked" rule is about. The cohort is small enough to inspect the list.
3. **What this supersedes, and what it keeps.** tasks-M3 **C38** ("`auction_min_bid = 0` is catalog-legal; solvency degenerates to ≥ 0; $0 openings legal; raises stay integer-increment") is **superseded as to its vehicle and promoted as to its substance.** The $0 floor is no longer a value of a 0–5 field — it is a named per-league toggle, defaulting OFF, that Chris ruled. C38's actual findings (a $0 floor is legal; raises stay `> high_bid`; the boundaries are pinned in pgTAP 034) are now spec law at §8.6.2/§8.6.3 and stay pinned. **Do not delete C38's pgTAP coverage** — re-point it at the toggle.
4. **The solvency machinery is not touched, weakened, or made conditional.** With the toggle ON it evaluates to `remaining ≥ 0` and stops firing; that is the correct outcome and §8.6.8 now says so in the LAW. A diff that *removes* an E28 arm, the insolvent-award refusal, or a solvency pin because "it can't fire with the toggle on" is an automatic review finding (tasks-M3 §4 rule 7, never-weaken class).
5. **The TS mirror moves with the SQL.** `auction-budget.ts`'s `minBid` becomes the same derived reserve, the SQL-parity fixture (D90 pattern) is extended to cover **both** toggle states, and the UI copy that says "minimum bid" stops saying it (`draft-lobby.tsx:342`, `settings-panel.tsx:1496`, and the E5 refusal wording, which should name the *reserve* rather than a "minimum bid").

### D199 — The uncontestable instant award

1. **The predicate, in SQL, as one query — never a loop.** After the opening bid row is written and `current_nomination` is set, one scan: `SELECT NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM public.teams t CROSS JOIN LATERAL public.draft_team_budget(v_draft.id, t.id) b WHERE t.league_id = … AND t.status <> 'retired' AND t.id <> v_nominator AND b.max_bid >= v_high_bid + v_increment)`. Same shape as `draft_auction_solvent` (`084:386`) and `draft_end` (`087:1004`), inside the same held draft-row lock. **`v_increment` is the literal `1`, named** — §8.6.9 requires `high_bid + increment`, not `> high_bid`, so the check stays correct if the increment ever becomes configurable; today they coincide and writing it the loose way would be a latent bug with no symptom.
2. **A complete roster needs no clause of its own.** `draft_team_budget` forces `max_bid = 0` at `open_slots <= 0` (`084:348–350`), and `high_bid + 1 ≥ 1 > 0`, so E27's teams are excluded by the money test. Keeping an explicit `b.open_slots >= 1` term is fine and is *documentation*; it must not be the thing the correctness argument rests on.
3. **The award is extracted, then reused — it is not reimplemented.** ARM 2.6(b)'s ~150 inline lines (`089:2480–2671`) become `draft_award_nomination_internal(p_draft_id UUID)`, moved **byte-for-byte** (the attribution lookup with its `player_id` + `voided_at IS NULL` discriminators, the §8.6.8 re-check at the award, the `draft_picks` write, the rotation scan, the completion call). `draft_tick` then calls it. This is exactly the extraction 089 already performed for `draft_system_nominate_internal` (`089:658`) and for the same reason — a second consumer appeared. **Prove the move was lossless the way 087/R367 proved its own: the pgTAP messages and goldens over the award path are unchanged, and they pass before the new caller is added.**
4. **The award happens in the nomination's transaction, and the 3 seconds is the room's beat.** Chris asked for *"a 3 second tick with the message"*. A tick-scheduled 3 seconds cannot be 3 seconds: the sweep is ≤5s, so it would land anywhere in 3–8s and differently every time. So the server awards immediately and **the client holds the message for exactly 3s** — identical on every screen, no new scheduling machinery, and no window in which a bid could arrive at a market that is closed. **The wire mechanism, decided so nobody improvises one:** the uncontestable path performs **two UPDATEs inside the one transaction** — first the ordinary nomination open, with `current_nomination` carrying an additive `"uncontested": true` key, then the award-and-advance. Both fire 088's `drafts` broadcast (`current_nomination` is in D134's payload — `088:66–80`), so every client sees the announcement and then the award, in order, in one commit. **No new column, no new event name, no new topic.** The additive key is the only spec-shape change (§12.3's printed `{player_id, high_bid, high_bidder_team_id}`) and rides the migration's banner as an erratum.
5. **Deliberately NOT done:** no scheduled server action, no pg_net, no second cron. If AP.3's reactive mechanism had needed one, this could have ridden it — it does not, and neither does AP.3 (D200(1)).

### D200 — Reactive CPU bidding: provoked, not swept

1. **The fix is to change *what provokes* a CPU, not *how often the sweep runs*.** A CPU response runs **in the transaction that provoked it**: a human's bid, another CPU's raise, and the nomination that opened the market. The award already advances the rotation, so a CPU whose turn it is to nominate can nominate in that same transaction too. What remains for the sweep is **expiring clocks when nobody acts** — the job it is good at and the only one that genuinely needs a timer. **The 5-second global `cron.schedule` at `068:1265` is not touched**, and ARM 2.6(a)/(b)/(c1) all stay as the safety net for the no-actor case.
2. **The extraction.** ARM 2.6(c2)'s candidate query + raise (`089:2313–2345`) becomes `draft_mock_cpu_respond_internal(p_draft_id UUID) RETURNS INTEGER` (the number of raises it wrote), moved verbatim first and *then* extended. `draft_place_bid_internal` (`089:512`) gains a mock arm that calls it after a successful write; the nomination paths (`draft_nominate` and `draft_system_nominate_internal`) call it once after opening. ARM 2.6(c2) becomes a call to the same internal, so the think-time path and the reactive path are **one implementation** — the 089 rule.
3. **Bounded, and bounded loudly.** The responder recurses through `draft_place_bid_internal`, so it needs a ceiling: **a hard cap of `2 × team_count` raises per provoking transaction**, and a `RAISE EXCEPTION` (not a silent `EXIT`) if it is ever reached — a ladder that long means the value model or the candidate query is wrong, and a silent stop would hide it. In practice jump-bids make the real ladder a handful of steps. The whole thing runs under the draft-row lock already held, so §4.6's held-lock budget applies: **measure it and record the number** (see AP.3's DoD).
4. **Jump-bids: a seeded choice between the jump and the nibble, never above value.** Let `ceiling := LEAST(value, max_bid)`. 5. **What this is worth, arithmetically, so the task can be judged against a number.** With reactive responses the mock's cost per nomination collapses to *the bid window plus at most one sweep interval* (the buzzer still belongs to the tick): `180 × (bid_window + ≤5s)`. At a 10s window it is ≈ **42 minutes**, under the bar. **So AP.3 alone does not meet the acceptance criterion; AP.3 + AP.4 do.** That dependency is stated here rather than discovered at the gate, and it is filed as **F85**.

   > **A claim about the state of the world after your change must name the command or observation that establishes it — the falsifiability floor of tasks-M1/M2/M3 §4 rule 3, applied to prose — *no pin counts as a pin until it has been shown RED against the defect it claims to catch*. "The other arms have buttons, so no state is a dead end" (four arms checked, two never enumerated — both had no exit at all). "The false docblock claim WAS corrected" (one of its two false sentences was). "`route-groups.test.ts` pins that" (pin written, path recalled — it is `src/lib/`, not `src/app/app/`). "Replayed with the identical cookie" (named in the helper, never delivered to `request.cookies`). Each was written in the *proved* register beside claims that genuinely were proved, and inherited their credibility; **each was one `grep`, one `wc -l`, or one DOM inventory away from being caught.** So, concretely: a sentence of the form *"X still lands"*, *"Y is not a dead end"*, *"Z was corrected"*, *"the pin covers it"* is **not shippable without the command or measurement beside it** — in the PR body, the docblock, or the PROGRESS entry. Prefer citing by **symbol** over line number where you can; a symbol survives the next edit. If you cannot cheaply establish it, write what you actually know ("not checked") — a hedge is free and a false disclosure costs the next Builder a day.

This lane exists because a plausible-looking arithmetic model (F76's) was right about the order of magnitude and nobody had timed the thing.
11. **The clock stays server-authoritative and the beat stays client-side.** Nothing in this lane may introduce a client-computed deadline, a client-decided award, or a server-side `pg_sleep`. §8.6.9's 3 seconds is a render delay over an already-committed transaction; if a Builder finds themselves wanting the server to wait, they have taken a wrong turn and should stop and raise it.
